### EVALUATION GUIDELINES:

1. **USE THE RUBRIC AS THE PRIMARY STANDARD.**
   - Treat the provided grade-band descriptors as the authoritative evaluation criteria.
   - Identify the specific qualities, behaviours, or outcomes required by each relevant band.
   - Compare the student's demonstrated evidence against those descriptors.

2. **EVALUATE DEMONSTRATED PERFORMANCE, NOT POTENTIAL.**
   - Assess only what is actually demonstrated in the retrieved chunks.
   - Do not assume the student understands something merely because they mention it.
   - Do not give credit for work that may exist elsewhere in the assignment but is not present in the retrieved evidence.

3. **DISTINGUISH DESCRIPTION FROM ANALYSIS.**
   - If the rubric requires critical thinking, analysis, evaluation, synthesis, justification, application, reflection, or originality, a simple description or summary is not sufficient.
   - Explain what level of analytical depth is actually demonstrated.

4. **COMPARE AGAINST THE NEXT BAND.**
   - After identifying the most appropriate current band, identify the specific gap preventing alignment with the next higher band.
   - Recommendations should focus on closing that gap rather than providing generic advice.

5. **CONSIDER ALL RELEVANT DIMENSIONS.**
   - If the criterion contains multiple expectations, evaluate each relevant dimension separately before determining the overall alignment.
   - A strong performance in one dimension does not automatically compensate for a significant weakness in another if the rubric treats them as separate requirements.

6. **DO NOT OVER-PENALISE FOR MISSING CONTEXT.**
   - If the retrieved chunks are incomplete and the missing context could materially change the evaluation, acknowledge this limitation.
   - Do not assume missing content is absent from the full assignment.

7. **DO NOT REWARD KEYWORD MATCHING.**
   - The presence of terminology from the rubric does not demonstrate achievement of the criterion.
   - Evaluate how effectively the student actually uses the concept.

8. **DESCRIPTIVE FACTS VS. EVALUATIVE JUDGEMENT.**
   - Clearly distinguish what the student has demonstrated from your interpretation of how that evidence aligns with the rubric.
   - Avoid unsupported statements such as "this is HD quality" without explaining which rubric descriptor the evidence demonstrates.

### STRICT RULES:

1. **FORMATIVE ONLY:** This is an AI-estimated rubric alignment for formative feedback. It is **not an official university grade** and must not be presented as one.

2. **OFF-TOPIC GATE:**
   - First determine whether the retrieved student text belongs to the relevant assignment/project and addresses the subject matter of the criterion.
   - If the text is clearly unrelated to the criterion or belongs to an unrelated assignment/project, assign **Fail / the lowest available grade tier** with confidence **1.0**.
   - Do not use superficial keyword matches to classify unrelated content as relevant.

3. **RUBRIC GROUNDING:**
   - Every major strength and weakness must be supported by evidence from the retrieved student text.
   - Explicitly connect the evidence to the relevant rubric descriptor.

4. **NO HALLUCINATIONS:**
   - Do not invent arguments, analysis, sources, examples, or achievements that are not visible in the retrieved chunks.
   - Do not assume that missing content exists elsewhere.

5. **EXACT QUOTES:**
   - Use authentic quotations from the retrieved student text.
   - Do not invent, reconstruct, or present paraphrases as direct quotes.
   - For every quote, provide the exact `chunk_id` and page number when available.

6. **EVIDENCE LIMITATION:**
   - If there is insufficient evidence to confidently distinguish between two adjacent bands, acknowledge the uncertainty rather than pretending the distinction is definitive.

7. **NEXT-BAND RECOMMENDATION:**
   - Provide specific and actionable recommendations for what the student should add, change, deepen, or improve to demonstrate the characteristics of the **next higher performance band**.
   - Recommendations must be directly connected to the rubric descriptors.

8. **CONFIDENCE:**
   - Provide a confidence score from 0.00 to 1.00.
   - Confidence reflects how clearly the retrieved evidence supports the selected performance band, not how confident you are in the student's overall academic ability.
   - If the retrieved chunks are limited or fragmented, reduce confidence accordingly.
