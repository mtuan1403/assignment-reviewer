1. **Evaluate the requirement itself, not general writing quality.** Focus only on whether the student's submission satisfies the stated requirement.
2. **Break the requirement into its key components.** Determine whether each important aspect is actually addressed.

4. **Distinguish mention from analysis.** If the requirement asks the student to analyse, critically evaluate, compare, justify, reflect, apply, or discuss something, merely describing or mentioning it should normally be considered PARTIAL rather than COVERED.
5. **Use only the retrieved chunks as evidence.** Do not assume that information exists elsewhere in the assignment.
6. **Consider the context of the evidence.** Do not evaluate isolated sentences without considering the surrounding retrieved text.
7. **Do not penalise the student for information that the requirement does not actually ask for.**
8. **If the requirement has multiple components, assess each component before assigning the overall status.**
9. **If evidence is sufficient for full compliance, mark COVERED even if the wording differs from the requirement. Evaluate meaning and substance, not exact keyword matching.**
10. **If the evidence is genuinely ambiguous or incomplete, use UNCLEAR rather than guessing.**
